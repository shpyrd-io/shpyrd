<Steps>

## The right people open the right apps.

An app restricted to a team, and a site anyone can open.

### Sign in with your company's login

A person signs in through single sign-on (SSO), where it is set up.

### Open the app from the portal

The portal lists the apps that person may open.

### Or open it to everyone

A public site needs no invitation: visitors just open it.

</Steps>

<Capabilities>

## What makes it work

### Public or sign-in required

Choose for each application.

### Company sign-in

Through supported identity providers.

### People, teams, and roles

Separate permissions to use an app, view its management information, publish changes, and administer.

### An app portal

One place where people find the apps they can use.

### Identity for your app

Who is signed in is made available to the application, for supported integrations.

</Capabilities>

<Questions>

## Practical questions

### Does access control what people can do inside the app?

No. Shpyrd controls who can open the app and tells it who they are. Actions inside it, like approving a payment, are your app's to define.

### Can we use our company login?

We can confirm SSO support for your identity provider and the offer that fits your setup.

### Can an app be open to the public?

Yes. Make it public and anyone with the link can open it.

</Questions>
