<Steps>

## A booking app, its data, and its background work.

Each part does a job the app needs.

### The app takes a booking

A web app or API serves the request.

### PostgreSQL keeps it

The booking is saved and read back later.

### A worker does the rest

A background process handles a task, such as sending a document to an external AI service.

</Steps>

<Capabilities>

## What a project can run

### Web apps and APIs

Your application, with or without a browser interface.

### Background workers

Processes that do work outside a request.

### PostgreSQL

A database for the information your app saves.

### Redis or Valkey

For compatible caching and queue use cases.

### Persistent storage

Volumes for the files your application keeps.

### Adjustable capacity

Connections, configuration, and sizes managed with the project.

</Capabilities>

<Questions>

## Practical questions

### Does a simple website need a database?

No. Add a database when your app needs to save information.

### Does capacity grow with traffic on its own?

Capacity is adjustable: you set the process counts and resource sizes your project needs.

### Does Shpyrd provide the booking or payment logic?

No. Shpyrd runs your application and its supported resources. Your code and the services you choose provide the business functionality.

</Questions>
